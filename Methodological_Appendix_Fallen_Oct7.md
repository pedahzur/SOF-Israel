The prologue rests on one ratio: sixty special operators among the three hundred sixty-nine security personnel who died in the fighting of October 7 and 8, 2023. This appendix documents the dataset behind that ratio.

## Data collection

The dataset covers the Israeli security personnel killed in the fighting of October 7 and 8, 2023, three hundred sixty-nine names in all. I compiled the list from Israel Defense Forces and Israel Police records and cross-checked every name against Kan public broadcasting's memorial coverage and the Mapping the Massacre project. ## Composition findings

The numbers tell a sharp story. The line that the attack overran was young and conscript. The force that turned the day was older, more professional, and more decorated. Officers made up a small share of the first line and a majority of the volunteers who came later. Reservists barely appear among the stationed dead and fill the ranks of the second wave. The pattern holds across every measure I can test.

The clock leaves its own signature. The men who died on the line died early, a few hours into the morning, where the attack found them. The men who came on their own died later, after the time it takes to wake, to understand, to arm, to drive, and to reach the fighting. The mean time from the start of the attack to the time of death runs roughly two hours later for the self-deployed volunteers than for the personnel stationed on the line. The gap between the two is the time the second wave needed to assemble itself out of private living rooms. No staff planned that mobilization. The country performed it on its own.

The special-operations share is the headline. Special operators amount to a fraction of a percent of the personnel under arms. Among the security dead of the first forty-eight hours they are roughly one in six. Read as a proxy, the ratio measures the speed and the mass of the special-operations response: the operators reached the worst places first and in strength, ahead of the system that should have deployed them.

## Limits

Three limits bound the dataset. The official records and the press coverage may misstate a unit affiliation, and several affiliations stay deliberately vague behind the classification line. The duty-status coding (stationed against self-deployed) rests in part on press reconstructions and family testimony rather than on official postings. And the count covers the security dead alone; it says nothing about the civilians of the response squads, whose fighting the prologue acknowledges but the dataset does not measure.
